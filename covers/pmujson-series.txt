The PMU event tables spell a field jevents.py reads by name two ways
wrongly: "BriefDescript6ion" in three AMD tables, and
"PublicDescrition" in the AmpereOneX mmu table.  jevents.py takes the
fields it wants out of each object and ignores the rest, so both were
dropped without a word, and the events went out with no description at
all.  perf list has shown them that way since v6.2 and v6.8.

Patches 1 and 2 are the data fixes.  They were posted on their own in
November and are unchanged here; they are 1/3 and 2/3 so that the tree
is right by the time 3/3 looks at it.

  https://lore.kernel.org/linux-perf-users/20261108184600.3810059-1-takano32@gmail.com/
  https://lore.kernel.org/linux-perf-users/20261108183846.3804875-1-takano32@gmail.com/

Patch 3 is new.  It notes which keys jevents.py looked at while
building an event and warns about any key in the object that nothing
looked at, naming the file, the key, and the number of objects with
it.  It warns rather than fails: a key nobody knows about in one
vendor's tables should not stop a build for an architecture that does
not use them.  JEVENTS_STRICT=1 asks for the error instead.

The generated tables are byte for byte unchanged on every
architecture, and the check costs about 0.2 seconds of the 5 the x86
tables take here.

Checked by running jevents.py over every architecture with and without
1 and 2; by building perf on arm64, once with the tables as they were
before 1 and 2 (the build succeeds and prints one warning per file) and
once with the whole series (silent); and by running mypy 1.19.1 on
jevents.py.
